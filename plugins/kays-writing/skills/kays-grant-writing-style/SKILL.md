# Grant proposal: Kays voice

Scope: Research proposals and specific-aims / project narratives for federal and agency funders (NSF, NASA, DoD/DoI agencies, foundations), including resubmissions.

Write persuasively and forward-looking, in active first-person plural ("We propose," "We will," "Our team"). Everything is framed around what will be done and why it matters, not what has been found.

Open the narrative by naming a specific, important limitation or gap in current knowledge, stated as a problem the field is stuck on. Then pivot with a clear "We propose to solve this problem by..." sentence that names the approach. State an overarching hypothesis explicitly, and lay out the science questions or specific aims as a short numbered list phrased as questions or objectives.

Make the intellectual payoff explicit and ambitious without hype: frame the work as unlocking, overcoming, or transforming, and tie it to why the answer matters for the field, for conservation, or for the funder's mission. Where a call has stated priorities, name them and show alignment directly.

Justify feasibility with concrete evidence that the team can deliver: prior work, existing infrastructure and datasets, published track record, permits, lab capacity, and preliminary results. Reference specific platforms, tools, models, and collaborators by name. When building on prior funding, briefly state the award, the outcome, and how it feeds this proposal.

Use rhetorical questions sparingly to expose a flawed assumption ("do we really expect a black bear in Florida to respond to temperature the same way as one in Maine?"), then answer them with the proposed work.

For resubmissions, open with a "Response to previous reviews" section: acknowledge the panel's enthusiasm by quoting their positive language briefly, then list the specific concerns and state point by point exactly how each was addressed, closing by noting the revisions preserve the original scope reviewers liked.

Organize with clear numbered or named sections (Background, Conceptual Framework and Aims, Approach and Methods, Deliverables, Broader Impacts). Include concrete deliverables and timelines where the funder expects them. Address broader impacts and outreach substantively, not as an afterthought.

Keep the technical approach precise but subordinate to the argument: enough method detail to convince a reviewer it will work, tied back to the aim it serves, without drifting into a full manuscript methods section.

Hedge on outcomes appropriately ("this approach might allow," "has the potential to") while staying confident about the plan and the team. Name contingency and alternative pathways where feasibility is a likely reviewer concern.

## Shared voice rules

Apply kays-voice-core to every draft (load it if it is not already loaded). It holds the
rules common to all of Roland's writing: the eight hedged-negation patterns, no
self-grading sentences, evidence in place of rigor claims, one-sentence limitations,
em dashes, length, and .docx delivery. Run its pre-delivery scan before handing over a draft.

Avoid: vague significance claims not tied to the funder's goals, methods described in isolation from the aim they serve, and burying feasibility evidence.
