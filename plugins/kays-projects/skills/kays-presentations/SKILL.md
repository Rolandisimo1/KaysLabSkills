# Making presentations for Roland

A talk is not a report with the prose deleted. The rules below come from
Roland's own teaching deck and from corrections he has made on real decks.

## 1. Storyline

The spine of a research talk:

1. Title slide, with coauthors
2. **Attention getter** -- never open with "X is an important topic"
3. Introduction to the broader topic; what we know, to build context
4. What is unknown in that field
5. The objective, addressing that unknown
6. Methods, kept brief
7. Results
8. Summary, tying back to the broader topic

**Acknowledgements go on the title slide or during methods**, not at the end.
A last-slide acknowledgement takes the room's attention away from the
conclusions.

Know the audience before choosing the level of detail. A collaborator meeting
and a conference session need different decks from the same results.

## 2. Slide design

- **Keep it simple. More visuals, fewer words.**
- **A picture full screen for a short time beats a quarter-screen picture for a
  long time.** When a figure is the point of the slide, give it the slide.
- Never write out what you plan to say. Text on a slide is for the few phrases
  the audience must read, not a script.
- Break a dense slide into two or three. If a slide needs a paragraph of
  explanation out loud, it is at least two slides.

### Showing several related figures

**Show one at full size first, talk through it, then show the rest.** A grid of
six panels on first appearance gives the audience nothing to look at. Lead with
one species, one panel, one example; once the audience knows how to read it,
the grid becomes a summary they can scan.

### Dark figures need dark slides

A black-background map on a white slide is a glare box. **When a figure has a
dark background, make the whole slide that same dark colour** and switch the
title and body type to a light tone. Never put a large dark image on white.

## 3. Figures for slides are not figures for print

This is the rule most often broken, because the print conventions are
well-practised and they are wrong here.

- **No caption block.** The small explanatory paragraph that belongs under a
  print figure is unreadable when projected, and it competes with the speaker.
  Whatever the caption would have said, either put it in the title in a few
  words or say it out loud. **Do not render caption text into a slide figure.**
- **Type is much larger than print.** Axis labels, tick labels, panel titles
  and legends all scale up. A figure that looks slightly oversized on screen is
  about right in a room.
- **Fewer panels per figure.** Split a nine-panel grid across several slides.
- **Label directly on the figure** where possible, so the eye does not travel
  to a legend.

Practical floors for a 13.3 x 7.5 inch slide: panel titles at least 16 pt in
figure units, tick and axis labels at least 13 pt, any annotation at least
12 pt. If a number is worth plotting it is worth reading from the back row.

## 4. Colour: the Linville Gorge palette

Sampled from autumn leaf litter. Earth tones, muted, works on both light and
dark slides.

    #1F1714  soil        darkest; dark-slide background
    #413530  bark        dark-slide panels, secondary background
    #5E5048  wet leaf    body type on light slides
    #95847A  dust        muted type, rules, de-emphasised series
    #C8BCB2  birch       light fills, light-slide background wash
    #E0DCD9  pale ash    lightest; body type on dark slides
    #649158  leaf green  positive change, increase
    #8D4652  maple red   negative change, decline
    #BDAFC4  lavender    categorical accent
    #B2A14A  yellow      highlight, caution, flagged results
    #936743  rust        categorical accent, warm series
    #B8889C  pink        categorical accent

**Use maple red for decline and leaf green for increase**, so direction reads
without a legend. Diverging maps run maple red to pale ash to leaf green.
Reserve yellow for the thing the audience is meant to question.

For a dark slide: soil background, pale ash type, dust for secondary type.
For a light slide: pale ash or white background, wet leaf type, dust secondary.

## 5. Results slides for this lab's work

- **Walk through one or two species in full before any fleet summary.** Every
  aspect for the example: trend, abundance surface, array map, covariate
  effects, stream agreement, convergence. Then the fleet-level view.
- **Choose example species with plenty of data and well-behaved fits.** A
  sparse species makes the method look worse than it is, and the diagnostics
  crowd out the result. Low-data and problem species get their own later
  section, where the point is the diagnostic rather than the estimate.
- **Name the species.** A slide that says "three species declined" with no names
  wastes the one thing the audience will remember.
- Do not show a result that has been superseded, and do not show a comparison
  that needs rescoring. Say what it will be instead.

## 6. Checklist before a deck is handed over

1. Any figure with a caption block rendered into it: regenerate without it.
2. Any text in a figure under about 12 pt at slide scale: regenerate larger.
3. Any dark figure on a light slide: flip the slide to dark.
4. Any slide carrying more than about six lines of text: split it.
5. Any multi-panel figure appearing before its single-panel introduction: add
   the lead-in slide.
6. Any species referred to by count rather than name: name them.
7. Palette applied, with red and green carrying direction.
